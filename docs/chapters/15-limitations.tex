\section{Known Limitations and Evolution}

The current identity header, simulated payment, category-level inventory,
process-local rate limiter/metrics, and polling expiry worker are deliberate
assessment trade-offs. Their operational impact and production evolution path
are recorded in \nolinkurl{docs/ASSUMPTIONS_SCOPE_LIMITATIONS.md}. None of these
limitations relaxes the critical invariants: inventory cannot oversell,
idempotent retries cannot double-book, voucher quota cannot over-redeem, and
expiry cannot double-release resources.

\section{Feature Scope: Voucher Operations}
\subsection{Assumption}
The assessment requires voucher behavior needed by the booking flow, not a full
campaign-management product.

\subsection{Implemented}
Operators/admins can create validated percentage or fixed vouchers with a time
window, global quota, and optional concert/category scope. Customers can apply
them atomically during booking; expiry/cancellation can release reserved quota.

\subsection{Not Implemented}
Voucher update, disable-through-API, delete, bulk import, campaign analytics,
and policy versioning endpoints are absent.

\subsection{Reason, Consequence, and Production Evolution}
Mutation/deletion after redemption needs explicit historical and authorization
rules, so it was omitted rather than implemented ambiguously. The consequence
is that an existing campaign cannot be administered through this API. A
production extension should add immutable voucher versions, audit every
administrative change, define disable semantics for existing reservations, and
protect elevated commands with production identity and approval policy.
