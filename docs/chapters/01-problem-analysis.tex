\section{Business Context}
The platform supports two workflow groups. Customers browse published
concerts, inspect category prices and availability, reserve finite inventory,
optionally apply a voucher, and track a booking through a deliberately small
lifecycle. Internal operators and administrators monitor bookings, investigate
failures or suspicious activity, perform guarded status changes, and maintain
the minimal concert/category/voucher setup implemented for the assessment.
The assessment brief describes this context.

The assessment describes about 50,000 users and a peak of 300--500 booking
requests per minute (approximately 5--8.3 requests per second). This throughput
is modest for PostgreSQL; the difficult property is synchronized demand for the
same last tickets or voucher units during a flash sale.

\section{Primary Engineering Risks and Design Goal}
\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Assessment risks and intended engineering response}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{p{0.23\textwidth}YY}
\toprule
\textbf{Risk} & \textbf{Failure mode} & \textbf{Design response} \\
\midrule
Overselling & More tickets sold than inventory & Atomic conditional inventory update inside a DB transaction \\
Duplicate retries & Multiple bookings from one user intent & Idempotency key, uniqueness, fingerprint, and response replay \\
Voucher abuse & Global/per-user quota exceeded & Atomic quota update and redemption constraint \\
Flash-sale instability & Retry storms and hot-row contention & Short transactions, controlled errors, rate limiting, and measured load evidence \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The design goal is a backend whose critical invariants remain correct under
concurrent and retried requests, with enough explicit evidence that a reviewer
can reproduce and defend each decision. Feature count is secondary to that
correctness objective.
